# FraudOS — AEGIS.AI · Impactful Panel Introduction

**For:** the opening 90-120 seconds in front of a presentation panel
**Goal:** establish credibility, frame the problem, promise the outcome, hand off cleanly into the rest of the talk
**Length:** ~220 words spoken at a measured pace

> Read **bold** lines verbatim. *Italics are stage directions — body language, eye contact, pacing.*

---

## The 30-second pre-flight protocol

*Before you say a word:*

1. **Walk up calmly.** Slow steps, shoulders back. The panel is reading your confidence before they hear it.
2. **Put your laptop / clicker down deliberately.** No fumbling.
3. **Look up.** Make eye contact with each panel member for one full second — left, centre, right. This is half a second of silence that says "I am ready, and so are you."
4. **One steady breath.** In through the nose, out through the mouth. Then begin.

*Do not start with "uh," "so," "um," or "okay so." Start with the first bold word below.*

---

## The opening — read this verbatim (90 seconds)

**"Good morning, members of the panel. Thank you for the time."**

*Pause for one beat. Look at the lead panellist.*

**"My name is [your name], and the project I am presenting today is FraudOS — AEGIS.AI. It is an AI-powered financial anomaly detection and reporting platform, built against the V1 Business Requirements Document authored by Yogesh Y, sponsored by Ashish Mehndi at Microsoft, and reviewed by Rajib Basu in Compliance. The platform is operated by a virtual specialist agent named Rahul, agent code AG-MS-0426-004, and is designed for use by the fraud and risk team of a fintech that processes five million transactions every single day."**

*Pause briefly. This is the headline.*

**"Here is the problem that started this project. Today, that fraud team flags transactions at a 92 percent false positive rate. Of every hundred transactions an investigator reviews, ninety-two are legitimate customers being delayed for no reason, and only eight are real fraud. That is a triple loss — investigators waste 92 percent of their day chasing noise, customers get frustrated by false declines, and real fraud is harder to spot because everyone is exhausted. The BRD's headline acceptance criterion, AC-02, asks us to bring that 92 percent figure to under 30 percent within four weeks of go-live, while never letting recall drop below 95 percent. Both numbers must move at the same time. We do not trade missed fraud for fewer false positives."**

*Beat.*

**"In the next twelve minutes I will walk you through four things. First, the business problem and the people it affects. Second, the platform — a hybrid machine-learning approach using Isolation Forest for point anomalies, LSTM for sequential patterns, and an ensemble score, paired with Azure OpenAI for plain-English investigator explanations. Third, a live demo of the seven screens that satisfy UI-01 through UI-07 of the BRD, including a real fraud confirmation and a threshold approval. And fourth, the measurable outcomes — the platform is currently tracking ahead of every quantifiable acceptance criterion in the document."**

*Slight pause, lift your eyes.*

**"Let us begin."**

*Click into the next slide / switch to the demo browser.*

## What just happened in those 90 seconds

You communicated nine things without sounding like a list:

1. Who you are (name + role implied by being the presenter).
2. The project name (FraudOS — AEGIS.AI).
3. The sponsor (Microsoft / Ashish Mehndi) — establishes legitimacy.
4. The author and reviewer of the BRD — shows you know who owns what.
5. The agent's name and code (Rahul / AG-MS-0426-004) — positions it as a real specified system, not a sketch.
6. The scale (5 million transactions per day) — makes it serious.
7. The headline business problem (92% FP rate) — the hook number.
8. The headline business outcome (under 30% in four weeks, recall ≥95%) — the promise number.
9. The shape of the rest of the talk — sets expectations so the panel knows what they are about to hear.

That is everything a panel needs to grant you full attention for the next twelve minutes.

## Three alternative openings — pick the one that fits the room

### Alternative A — for a senior business / executive panel

> **"Good morning. I am [your name]. The single most important number in the brief I am presenting today is 92 percent — that is the false positive rate the fraud team is fighting today. By the end of the next twelve minutes, you will see how the FraudOS platform brings that number to under 30 percent inside four weeks, without losing a single percentage point of recall on real fraud. The platform was built against Microsoft's V1 BRD authored by Yogesh Y and sponsored by Ashish Mehndi. Let me show you how."**

*Use when the audience is heavy on Heads-of and C-suite. Cuts the technical name-dropping and leads with the bottom-line impact.*

### Alternative B — for a technical / architecture review panel

> **"Good morning. I am [your name], and I am presenting FraudOS — AEGIS.AI, the V1 build of Microsoft's AI-powered financial anomaly detection platform per the BRD authored by Yogesh Y. The system processes 5 million transactions a day through a two-model hybrid — Isolation Forest for point anomalies and LSTM for sequential patterns, combined with a configurable ensemble score per BR-001. Flagged transactions are explained in plain English by Azure OpenAI GPT-4, grounded strictly on row attributes per BR-003, and reviewed by an investigator under BR-004 before any customer-facing action is taken. We satisfy every UI-01 through UI-07 component, and we are tracking ahead of every measurable acceptance criterion. Let me walk you through it."**

*Use when the panel is engineering-heavy and wants the architecture in the first thirty seconds.*

### Alternative C — for a mixed academic / capstone panel

> **"Good morning, members of the panel. I am [your name], and the project I am presenting is called FraudOS — AEGIS.AI. It is built against a real-world Business Requirements Document — Microsoft's V1 BRD for an AI-powered financial anomaly detection and reporting system. The brief from Microsoft was simple to state and hard to deliver: take a fraud detection pipeline that today flags transactions at a 92 percent false positive rate, and bring that figure to under 30 percent within four weeks, without missing a single percent of real fraud. Over the next twelve minutes I will show you the platform we built to do that — the technology choices, the screens an investigator uses, a live demonstration of a fraud confirmation, and the measurable outcomes against every acceptance criterion in the BRD. Thank you for the time."**

*Use when the panel is academic / capstone-style and includes external examiners. Establishes the project as grounded in a real industry brief.*

## Things to AVOID saying in the opening

- *"Hi everyone, so basically..."* — kills authority instantly.
- *"This is just my project on..."* — diminishes the work.
- *"I hope you like it."* — never apologise or seek approval pre-emptively.
- *"I'm a bit nervous, so..."* — never name nerves; everyone has them, you do not need to broadcast.
- *"My team and I have been working really hard on..."* — effort is irrelevant; outcome is everything.
- *"As you can see on the slide..."* — pointing at slides is the panel's job, not yours.
- *"I'll try to explain..."* — say "I will explain," never "I will try."
- Filler words: *uh, um, like, you know, basically, actually, sort of, kind of.* If you catch yourself starting one, just pause silently for half a second instead.

---

## Body language for the opening

- **Stand still.** Pacing reads as anxiety. Plant your feet shoulder-width apart and let them stay there.
- **Hands visible.** Either at your sides or holding the clicker / a pen. No pockets. No clasping behind your back.
- **One gesture per beat.** When you say "first," "second," "third," "fourth" — count them on your fingers. Visual anchoring helps the panel track structure.
- **Eye contact rotation.** Lock onto one panellist for the duration of one full sentence, then move to the next. Never sweep your eyes across the room.
- **Smile briefly at the headline number.** Not a grin — a small "this is the part you should remember" smile. It signals confidence in the number.

---

## Voice and pacing

- **Speak about 20% slower than feels natural.** Adrenaline speeds you up; you have to fight it.
- **Pause after every period.** Half a second of silence after a sentence is gold — it lets the point land.
- **Drop your voice at the end of sentences, not up.** Rising intonation makes statements sound like questions and undermines authority.
- **Volume slightly louder than conversation.** The back row of the panel needs to hear you without straining.

## The transition line into your next section

When you finish "Let us begin," do not pause for applause or reaction. Move immediately into your next slide or the demo browser. The transition phrase you can use:

> *"To start, here is what is at stake for the people who use this platform every day."*

That line bridges directly into section 1 of [presentation_script.md](presentation_script.md) (The problem we are solving) without a gap. Or if you are jumping straight to the demo:

## A 30-second emergency version

If the panel is running late and the chair gives you "two minutes max":

> **"Good morning. I am [your name]. I am presenting FraudOS — AEGIS.AI, an AI-powered fraud detection platform built against Microsoft's V1 BRD. The headline target: take the false positive rate from 92 percent to under 30 percent in four weeks, without losing recall on real fraud. Every screen you are about to see is a UI component the BRD specifies. Let me start with the demo."**

*That is 65 words, ~25 seconds spoken. Then go straight to the live demo.*
